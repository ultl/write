In some countries, celebrities complain about the way media publicize their privacy lives. Some people say that they should accept it as part of their fame. Do you agree or disagree?

The media's excessive intrusion of celebrity's privacy has long been rooted from either fame or notoriousness. Despite the fact that these high-profile people are told to accept inevitable consequences of being popular, public interest should have drawn boundaries between what can be crossed and what should be put limits on.

On the one hand, the toleration of media scrunity may be a sunk cost that any public figure initially accept, as a head nod to enter the industry full of dramas. Regardless of this tradeoff, constantly making the public curious about one's private life appears to be quite effective to get fame. This tactic is exactly how some major entertainment marketing operates. Agencies often rely on a highly calculated "buzz first, product second" blueprint to guarantee a built-in audience before the artistic work ever drops. When deploying this grab-public-spotlight formula, agencies often rely on so-called mechanic - controlled controversy. A typical example of this is the high-profile love triangle controversy surrounding pop start Son Tung M-TP, his rumoured long-term girlfriend, and his newly signed actress or MV protagonist generating massive public drama arround his major music drops. Probably, this can be hardly seen as a coinincidence.

On the other hand, I insist that no celebrities should have borne themselves silently when the media cross reasonable boundaries. Chief among the reasons is that privacy is a fundamental human right for any human being and should not disappear as a result of fame. Imagine being constantly surveiled by paparazzi, 24/7, even during family outings or outside one’s home, no one on Earth can stand against having psychological pressures or worse, mental issues. Either way, thereby, diminish their own overal well-beings. Another point worth mentioning is that rather than exploiting private scandals on journalism, media should focus on celebrities' professional talents, achievements and contribution to the society. Unless one's professionalism is prioritized over scandals, public figures are still unfairly treated as some pure talents can be ignored because of their lack of a well-prepared media training.

In conclusion, although celebrities should expect public attention, particularly when they use personal publicity to advance their careers, fame does not justify unlimited intrusion. They should retain the right to protect their private lives and be recognised primarily for their professional achievements.
